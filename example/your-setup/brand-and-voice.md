# Brand and voice

Example setup for Acme Deals, a fictional brand. The voice rules come from
`source-docs/voice-rules.md` (Nick Christensen, MIT).

## Who you are

- **Brand or your name:** Acme Deals
- **What you write about:** Running a small agency or a one-person marketing team at a profit: owning your software, reselling tools to clients, cutting overhead, getting reporting done faster.
- **Who you write for (your reader):** Solo and small agency owners with 5 to 15 client retainers, and the only marketer at a small company. They buy their own tools and count every recurring bill.

## Voice in three words

Direct, practical, operator to operator

## How you sound

One operator talking to another. Short sentences, second person, no warm-up. Lead with the
outcome or the job to be done, not the product name. Use the customer's exact words, grammar
and slang included. Proof over claims: name the mechanism, show the number, say where it
came from. One idea per piece. Clear over clever, and no humour that needs explaining.

## Words and phrases you avoid

- disruptive, game-changing, revolutionary, leverage (as a verb), synergy, seamless, best-in-class
- guru, ninja, rockstar, unlock, growth hacks, explosive growth, secret formula
- "anyone can do this", "no experience needed", "in just minutes"

## Hard rules

- Never use em dashes. Use a comma, full stop, or colon instead.
- No hashtags in body copy. No emoji.
- Never promise a result the reader has not been shown the math for.
- Social posts: the link goes in the first comment, not in the caption.
- The read-aloud test: if it sounds like a brand "communicating", rewrite it.

## Your voice examples

The real source of your voice is the articles in `voice-examples/`. This file captures the
rules; the examples capture the feel. When they disagree, the examples win.

What the two examples have in common:

- They open on the reader's situation in one or two short sentences, then state the claim.
- Every section header is a question the reader would ask, and the first line under it answers it.
- Customer quotes are used whole, with the person's role, and never tidied up.
- Numbers come as worked examples the reader can redo with their own figures, and are labelled as examples.
- They end on one next step, not a summary.

The sample newsletter (`newsletter-monday-reporting.md`) is shorter: a subject line that names what is inside, one quote, one idea in bold, one thing to try today, one link.
